# Where the Court states its signs of abuse: the Reasonableness judgment read (issue 13)

**What this is.** A research note on issue 13, with a bearing on issue 11. Issue 13 asks whether the High Court states, in its own words, the four signs that the project takes from Medina's account of the Court's guideline (I·CON 2026, p. 699), and whether it treats them together as a presumption of abuse. The four signs are:
- the amendment serves a specific interest of the sitting government (the beneficiary sign);
- it takes effect immediately;
- it passed by coalition votes only;
- it was expedited.

**Why it matters.** The project's prediction table, in the main note's revised T1 (the blind test of whether the Court's rulings follow the signs), predicts "Adverse, as a rebuttable presumption of abuse" when all four are present, citing p. 699. Adverse is the project's outcome class for a ruling that went against the law in any degree: struck down, deferred, warned against or read narrowly. Issue 13's check is to find the passages where the Court lists the signs, in four judgments:
- HCJ 8260/16 (*Academic Center*, 2017);
- HCJ 5969/20 (*Shafir*, 2021);
- HCJ 2412/23 (Incapacity, 3 January 2024);
- HCJ 5658/23 (Reasonableness, 1 January 2024).

**What was read.** The Versa translation of HCJ 5658/23, *Movement for Quality Government in Israel v. Knesset* (https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/movement-quality-government-israel-v-knesset), read on 5 November 2026. It translates President (emer.) Hayut's opinion only, which ends at para. 214. The other justices' opinions are not translated there.

## What the Reasonableness judgment says

**1. The Court's own test is the two-stage test from *Shafir* (para. 58).**

- **Stage 1, identification.** Hayut summarises her test in *Shafir*: the Court asks whether the amendment bears the formal hallmarks of a constitutional norm. "Several supplementary tests were established, which do not form a closed list":
  - stability;
  - generality, "which addresses whether the norm has general, abstract application that relates to a non-specific group, as opposed to a personal norm";
  - compatibility with the constitutional fabric.

  `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 58]`
- **Stage 2, justification.** If the amendment fails one or more of these tests, "the burden shifts to the respondents to show a special justification". `[attested: as above]`
- **The vote in *Shafir*.** Six of nine justices held that the amendment lacked the characteristics of a constitutional norm and that the Knesset had abused its constituent power. `[attested: as above]`

**2. Three of the four signs appear together, under the generality test (para. 192).**

- **The beneficiary.** "A personal norm may be directed at a specific person as well as an institutional 'persona' like the Knesset or a particular Government." Of the amendment, she writes: "Thus, the Amendment exclusively benefits the Government and its ministers." `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 192]`
- **Immediate effect.** "The immediate entry into force of a norm does not absolutely rule out its identification as a constitutional norm, but it may sometimes raise a problem as to its generality." For this she cites the Rotation Government case (HCJ 2905/20) and *Scheinfeld* (HCJ 8948/22). `[attested: as above]`
- **Coalition votes.** The Government, the amendment's "sole beneficiary", worked to "approve it by means of the coalition majority that it enjoys in the Knesset". `[attested: as above]`
- **Her conclusion.** These features "can, indeed, raise a concern that it is intended to serve the narrow interests of a particular political majority". Then: "I do not see any need to make a definitive ruling on the question of whether the problems noted above rise to the level of an abuse of constituent power in the present matter". She voided the amendment on other grounds: that the Knesset exceeded its constituent power by the amendment's content. `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, paras. 192 and 214]`

**3. The fourth sign, haste, is treated apart, as a defect in the legislative process (paras. 193–195).**

- She quotes *Scheinfeld*: "it would be better that amendments to Basic Laws not be advanced hastily and on a fast track". `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 194]`
- Of this amendment she writes that "the legislative process took only about a month". `[attested: as above]`
- She weighs this under the principle that Knesset members must be able to take part in the legislative process, not under abuse of constituent power. She holds that the high threshold for intervening on that ground "was not crossed". `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 195]`

**4. Two further passages bear on the project.**

- **The pattern the doctrine responds to.** Hayut quotes her own opinion in the Rotation Government case: regime changes "executed ad hoc, sometimes by means of temporary provision, for immediate implementation (sometimes exclusively) by the Knesset that executed them". `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 59]`
- **Deferral as a remedy.** She notes that some petitioners raised "the remedy of postponed application, which was noted as an alternative remedy on the basis of the doctrine of abuse of constituent power". She held it insufficient for a defect in content. `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 182]` Two days later, deferral was the remedy the Court gave in the Incapacity case. `[attested: research/frame/challenges.json]`

## What this shows, and what it does not

- **In this judgment the signs are not stated as a set or as a presumption.** Three of the four (beneficiary, immediate effect, coalition votes) appear in one paragraph, as considerations within one of *Shafir*'s identification tests, generality, and they "raise a concern". The fourth, haste, is assessed separately and found not to cross its threshold. The question of abuse is expressly left undecided. This is a weighing, not a conjunction. `[conjectural]`
- **A presumption does exist in the Court's doctrine, but it has a different trigger.** It is *Shafir*'s second stage: failing any one identification test shifts the burden of justification to the respondents. That is a rebuttable presumption triggered by one failed test. It is not triggered by four signs together. `[conjectural]`
- **This is one of the four judgments issue 13 names, and only one opinion in it.** The likeliest source of Medina's statement at p. 699 is the Incapacity judgment, HCJ 2412/23, decided two days later. That is the only case in which the Court found an abuse of constituent power in a personal amendment and deferred it. `[conjectural]` That judgment was not read:
  - Versa has no translation of it (searched by case number and by "incapacitation" on 5 November 2026).
  - The Israel Democracy Institute's copy of the full judgment, cited in research/frame/challenges.json, is on www.idi.org.il, which this session's network blocks.
  - The Judiciary's English site, supremedecisions.court.gov.il, is blocked too.
- **Medina's own footnote at p. 699 would say which judgment he draws on.** This note has not seen it.

## Effect on the project

- **Issue 13 is not yet settled, but the evidence so far points one way.** Issue 13's stopping rule: if the Court's list differs (fewer signs, a weighing rather than a conjunction, or different thresholds), the difference is a criticism of the test's design and is logged, and the prediction table is not changed until the note says how. In the one judgment read, it differs in exactly those respects. The rule asks for the passages in all four judgments, so issue 13 stays open until HCJ 2412/23 is read and Medina's footnote at p. 699 is checked. `[conjectural]`
- **The main note's wording stands for now.** The note says Medina "reports" the Court stating the four signs together (p. 699). It labels the table "Medina's doctrine, as this project operationalizes it", and it already says the table hardens Medina's "likely" into a definite prediction. If the Incapacity judgment also lacks the set, the change belongs in the main note's change log, decided by the author. `[conjectural]`
