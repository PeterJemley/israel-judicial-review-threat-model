# Where the Court states its signs of abuse: the Reasonableness judgment read (issue 13)

**What this is.** A research note on issue 13, with a bearing on issue 11. Issue 13 asks whether the High Court states, in its own words, the four signs that the project takes from Medina's account of the Court's guideline (I·CON 2026, p. 699), and whether it treats them together as a presumption of abuse. The four signs are:
- the amendment serves a specific interest of the sitting government (the beneficiary sign);
- it takes effect immediately;
- it passed by coalition votes only;
- it was expedited.

**Why it matters.** The project's prediction table, in the main note's revised T1 (the blind test of whether the Court's rulings follow the signs), predicts "Adverse, as a rebuttable presumption of abuse" when all four are present, citing p. 699. Adverse is the project's outcome class for a ruling that went against the law in any degree: struck down, deferred, warned against or read narrowly. Issue 13's check is to find the passages where the Court lists the signs, in four judgments:
- HCJ 8260/16 (*Academic Center*, 2017);
- HCJ 5969/20 (*Shafir*, 2021);
- HCJ 2412/23 (Incapacity, 3 January 2024);
- HCJ 5658/23 (Reasonableness, 1 January 2024).

**What was read.** The Versa translation of HCJ 5658/23, *Movement for Quality Government in Israel v. Knesset* (https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/movement-quality-government-israel-v-knesset), read on 5 November 2026. It translates President (emer.) Hayut's opinion only, which ends at para. 214. The other justices' opinions are not translated there.

## What the Reasonableness judgment says

**1. The Court's own test is the two-stage test from *Shafir* (para. 58).**

- **Stage 1, identification.** Hayut summarises her test in *Shafir*: the Court asks whether the amendment bears the formal hallmarks of a constitutional norm. "Several supplementary tests were established, which do not form a closed list":
  - stability;
  - generality, "which addresses whether the norm has general, abstract application that relates to a non-specific group, as opposed to a personal norm";
  - compatibility with the constitutional fabric.

  `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 58]`
- **Stage 2, justification.** If the amendment fails one or more of these tests, "the burden shifts to the respondents to show a special justification". `[attested: as above]`
- **The vote in *Shafir*.** Six of nine justices held that the amendment lacked the characteristics of a constitutional norm and that the Knesset had abused its constituent power. `[attested: as above]`

**2. Three of the four signs appear together, under the generality test (para. 192).**

- **The beneficiary.** "A personal norm may be directed at a specific person as well as an institutional 'persona' like the Knesset or a particular Government." Of the amendment, she writes: "Thus, the Amendment exclusively benefits the Government and its ministers." `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 192]`
- **Immediate effect.** "The immediate entry into force of a norm does not absolutely rule out its identification as a constitutional norm, but it may sometimes raise a problem as to its generality." For this she cites the Rotation Government case (HCJ 2905/20) and *Scheinfeld* (HCJ 8948/22). `[attested: as above]`
- **Coalition votes.** The Government, the amendment's "sole beneficiary", worked to "approve it by means of the coalition majority that it enjoys in the Knesset". `[attested: as above]`
- **Her conclusion.** These features "can, indeed, raise a concern that it is intended to serve the narrow interests of a particular political majority". Then: "I do not see any need to make a definitive ruling on the question of whether the problems noted above rise to the level of an abuse of constituent power in the present matter". She voided the amendment on other grounds: that the Knesset exceeded its constituent power by the amendment's content. `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, paras. 192 and 214]`

**3. The fourth sign, haste, is treated apart, as a defect in the legislative process (paras. 193–195).**

- She quotes *Scheinfeld*: "it would be better that amendments to Basic Laws not be advanced hastily and on a fast track". `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 194]`
- Of this amendment she writes that "the legislative process took only about a month". `[attested: as above]`
- She weighs this under the principle that Knesset members must be able to take part in the legislative process, not under abuse of constituent power. She holds that the high threshold for intervening on that ground "was not crossed". `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 195]`

**4. Two further passages bear on the project.**

- **The pattern the doctrine responds to.** Hayut quotes her own opinion in the Rotation Government case: regime changes "executed ad hoc, sometimes by means of temporary provision, for immediate implementation (sometimes exclusively) by the Knesset that executed them". `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 59]`
- **Deferral as a remedy.** She notes that some petitioners raised "the remedy of postponed application, which was noted as an alternative remedy on the basis of the doctrine of abuse of constituent power". She held it insufficient for a defect in content. `[attested: Versa, HCJ 5658/23, para. 182]` Two days later, deferral was the remedy the Court gave in the Incapacity case. `[attested: research/frame/challenges.json]`

## What this shows, and what it does not

- **In this judgment the signs are not stated as a set or as a presumption.** Three of the four (beneficiary, immediate effect, coalition votes) appear in one paragraph, as considerations within one of *Shafir*'s identification tests, generality, and they "raise a concern". The fourth, haste, is assessed separately and found not to cross its threshold. The question of abuse is expressly left undecided. This is a weighing, not a conjunction. `[conjectural]`
- **A presumption does exist in the Court's doctrine, but it has a different trigger.** It is *Shafir*'s second stage: failing any one identification test shifts the burden of justification to the respondents. That is a rebuttable presumption triggered by one failed test. It is not triggered by four signs together. `[conjectural]`
- **This is one of the four judgments issue 13 names, and only one opinion in it.** The likeliest source of Medina's statement at p. 699 is the Incapacity judgment, HCJ 2412/23, decided two days later. That is the only case in which the Court found an abuse of constituent power in a personal amendment and deferred it. `[conjectural]` That judgment was not read:
  - Versa has no translation of it (searched by case number and by "incapacitation" on 5 November 2026).
  - The Israel Democracy Institute's copy of the full judgment, cited in research/frame/challenges.json, is on www.idi.org.il, which this session's network blocks.
  - The Judiciary's English site, supremedecisions.court.gov.il, is blocked too.
- **Medina's own footnote at p. 699 would say which judgment he draws on.** This note has not seen it.

## Addendum, 8 November 2026: the Incapacity judgment read (HCJ 2412/23)

**What was read.** The full judgment in HCJ 2412/23 and HCJ 2466/23, *Movement for Quality Government in Israel v. Knesset*, 3 January 2024: 212 pages, in Hebrew. It was read from the Israel Democracy Institute's copy (https://www.idi.org.il/media/22632/full-verdict-of-supreme-court-regarding-incapacitation-of-prime-minister-january-3-2024.pdf), downloaded on 8 November 2026 once the author allowed the host. No English translation was found; Versa has none. The copy is not stored in this repository.

**How it is quoted.** Each passage is given in the Hebrew original, with this note's own English rendering. The renderings are unofficial. Where a point turns on a word, the Hebrew governs.
- `[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, opinion and para.; rendering this note's]`

### Who held what

- **The operative order is a single holding.** The case was "decided by a majority (Hayut, Vogelman, Amit, Barak-Erez, Baron and Grosskopf), against the dissent of Sohlberg, Mintz, Elron, Willner and Stein, as stated in the judgment of the Acting President". In Hebrew: "הוחלט ברוב דעות ... כנגד דעתם החולקת של ... כאמור בפסק דינו של מ"מ הנשיא". `[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, operative paragraph; rendering this note's]`
- **The lead opinion** is Acting President Vogelman's. He holds that "a series of clear and unequivocal indications" shows the amendment's purpose was personal, so it fails the generality test (para. 56). The remedy defers it to the next Knesset. `[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, Vogelman, para. 56; rendering this note's]`

### Personal purpose, opinion by opinion (issue 11)

There was no separate vote on personal purpose, but every opinion takes a position on it.

- **Six found a personal purpose:** the majority.
- **Four found none:**
  - Sohlberg: the inquiry should have stopped at the text, "free of any dimension of personality", and held the amendment general. Even on the wider approach, "there is no evidence before us of a personal arrangement" (para. 74).
  - Mintz: "it is not a personal amendment in our case" ("לא מדובר בענייננו בתיקון פרסונלי", para. 49).
  - Elron: even on the approach of the doctrine's supporters, "I do not think the amendment's purpose is distinctly personal" ("איני סבור שתכלית התיקון היא פרסונלית באופן מובהק", para. 3).
  - Stein: he accepts that the law's initiators also wished to protect Netanyahu from a declaration of incapacity (para. 5), but holds that "such a law cannot be considered personal" ("חוק כזה אינו יכול להיחשב לפרסונלי", para. 12).
- **One did not decide:** Willner. She holds that generality concerns the norm's application, not the personal considerations of the members of the Knesset who voted for it. "Even if personal considerations stood before the members of the Knesset" in enacting it, that "does not change this conclusion" (para. 2).

`[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, each opinion at the paragraph given; renderings this note's]` The count, six for, four against and one holding the question irrelevant, is this note's reading of the opinions. `[conjectural]`

### The signs (issue 13)

**In the lead opinion (Vogelman):**
- **An open list of indications.**
  - A personal purpose may be identified from "the totality of the circumstances of the legislation".
  - "Hasty and rushed legislation may be an indication of a personal purpose" ("חקיקה נמהרת וחפוזה עשויה להוות אינדיקציה לקיומה של תכלית פרסונלית").
  - The timing may raise a personal "suspicion".
  - The content may show it, especially where it is "tailored to the measurements" of a specific person.
  - "This is not a 'closed list' of indications" ("אין מדובר ב'רשימה סגורה' של אינדיקציות").

  `[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, Vogelman, para. 54; rendering this note's]`
- **Immediate effect.**
  - "Immediate application of a Basic Law does not, in itself, establish that there was an abuse of constituent power." The legislature's intent about when the law applies may, however, show its purpose (para. 53).
  - But "where a Basic Law concerns the prime minister, immediate application raises, in and of itself, a suspicion of personality, since immediate application means application to one person whose identity is known" ("תחולה מידית מעוררת, מניה וביה, חשד לפרסונליות", para. 78).

  `[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, Vogelman, paras. 53 and 78; rendering this note's]`
- **Which way the presumption runs.** "The claim that a Basic Law does not meet the generality requirement undermines its presumption of constitutionality, and must therefore rest on a firm and substantial foundation" ("חותרת תחת חזקת חוקתיותו ומשכך עליה להישען על תשתית איתנה וממשית"). The presumption here favours the law. `[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, Vogelman, para. 56; rendering this note's]`
- **Coalition votes** appear once, in a general remark, not as an indication applied to this amendment. In the last decade, he writes, the Knessets enacted and amended Basic Laws dozens of times, "sometimes in expedited procedures relying on the votes of coalition members only" ("לעתים בהליכים מזורזים שנשענים על קולות חברי הקואליציה בלבד"). `[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, Vogelman, para. 93; rendering this note's]`

**In two other majority opinions:**
- **Hayut:** "the hurried manner in which the amendment was advanced, and the timing of the completion of the legislative process, also give the amendment the marks of an amendment intended for a personal purpose". She notes an expedited process of about a month ("האופן הבהול שבו קודמה הצעת התיקון ... נותנים בתיקון סימנים כתיקון שנועד לתכלית פרסונאלית"). `[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, Hayut, para. 6; rendering this note's]`
- **Grosskopf, on the two stages.** He restates *Shafir*'s two stages: first a "suspicious characteristic", from a list that is not closed; then justification (para. 13).
- **Grosskopf, on a presumption.** He proposes "a presumption that the active application of a substantive change with a distinct personal implication requires justification" ("נכון לקבוע חזקה לפיה החלה אקטיבית של שינוי מהותי בעל השלכה פרסונלית מובהקת מחייבת צידוק"). That presumption, he writes, "can be seen as a further category of suspicious characteristic, or as a subset of the characteristic of lack of generality". He reads Vogelman's opinion as implying it "between the lines" (para. 18).

`[attested: HCJ 2412/23, Hebrew original, Grosskopf, paras. 13 and 18; rendering this note's]`

### What the two judgments show together

- **Not a set.** The four signs do not appear as a set to be counted in either judgment. The question in both is whether the amendment has a personal purpose (the generality test), answered from an open list of indications weighed together. `[conjectural]`
- **Immediate effect and haste** are indications of a personal purpose in both judgments. Immediate effect never suffices by itself. `[conjectural]`
- **Coalition votes.** In the Reasonableness judgment, Hayut treats the coalition majority as sharpening a generality concern (para. 192). In the Incapacity judgment's lead opinion they appear only in a general remark (para. 93). Neither opinion treats them as an independent sign. `[conjectural]`
- **The only presumption against an amendment is Grosskopf's proposal**, in a concurring opinion. Two features, immediate application and a distinct personal implication, shift the burden of justification. *Shafir*'s second stage likewise shifts the burden once any one suspicious characteristic is found. The lead opinion speaks of a presumption of constitutionality, which the challenger must overcome. `[conjectural]`
- **What this means for Medina's guideline.** As the project states it (p. 699), it is not in the words of either judgment. It may be Medina's synthesis of these opinions: the lead opinion's indications, Grosskopf's presumption and the remarks on coalition votes. His footnote at p. 699 would show what he cites, and it has not been seen. `[conjectural]`

## Addendum, 9 November 2026: Medina's p. 699 read

**Source.** Images of pp. 698–699 of Medina (2026) that the author supplied on 9 November 2026. `[attested: Medina, I·CON 24 (2026), p. 699]`

**What the page says.**
- Medina writes that the Court "has addressed this challenge by establishing guidelines to detect abuse of power". He gives them as "examining the motives behind the amendment, its impact on partisan interests, the nature of the legislative process, and the form of the amendment".
- He then writes: "Specifically, an amendment addressing a specific interest of the current government, which takes immediate effect, is supported only by coalition members, and is enacted through an expedited process, is likely to be classified as serving an illegitimate purpose—and thus as an abuse of power—unless proven otherwise."
- There is no footnote on the page, and no judgment is cited for that sentence. The next paragraph gives the 2017 budget case as "one example".

**What it shows.**
- Medina presents the four signs as the specific form of guidelines the Court established. He does not quote the Court for them.
- His sentence states a sufficient condition: all four signs together make an illegitimate purpose likely, unless the government shows otherwise. That is compatible with the two judgments read above, where all four would be strong indications of a personal purpose and would shift the burden of justification. `[conjectural]`
- The difference logged under issue 13 therefore lies mainly in the project's use of the sentence, not in the sentence itself. The project counts the signs (F), and treats a low count as the absence of an abusive purpose, which is its own threshold. The Court allows one indication, weighed with the rest, to suffice. `[conjectural]`

**Applied.** The main note now quotes Medina's words and no longer says he "reports the Court stating" the signs (change log (bj); errors list, entry 87).

**Also on p. 698, bearing on issue 12 (Hasson).**
- Medina writes that "in a controversial decision, the Court avoided applying this doctrine to the specific case, opting instead to interpret Article 7 in a way that rendered it ineffective". As a result, he says, Article 7 "was interpreted as not exempting the government from the duty to treat all citizens as equals, including in the subject matters it covers". `[attested: Medina, p. 698]`
- This is Medina's reading of the judgment, which agrees with the codebook's coding of row 7 as narrowed. Issue 12's stopping rule asks for the judgment itself. `[conjectural]`
