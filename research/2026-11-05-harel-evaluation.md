# Harel (Haaretz, 4 November 2026) evaluated through the project, with the project's predictions recorded before they resolve

**What "the project" is.** This repository tests a sentence in Barak Medina's 2026 article in the *International Journal of Constitutional Law* (I·CON), p. 685. The sentence says the 2023 judicial plan posed "an imminent risk to Israel's existence as a democracy, and, in fact, to Israel's very existence", and that these are "not threats to private interests or ideologies of the liberal elite". The project reads that sentence through the threat-assessment formula Threat = Capability × Intent: what an actor can do, multiplied by what it means to do. The main document, "the note", is [medina-threat-conjecture-note.md](../medina-threat-conjecture-note.md); the [README](../README.md) is the plain-language entry point. Labels the project uses are explained where they first appear.

**Source.** Amos Harel, "Netanyahu Is Plotting a Final Power Grab for Israel's Democracy. His Loyalist Shin Bet Chief Is the Key", Haaretz (English), "Analysis", 4 November 2026, 18:40 IST. Read in full from a copy the project's author saved on 5 November 2026 at 07:52. The copy is not stored in this repository.

**Status.** A research note only, written at the project author's direction on 5 November 2026. Nothing here is applied to the note. Claims from unnamed sources are leads until a second outlet or an official record confirms them. Corrected on 5 November 2026 after an outside reader's review (see the main note's change log, item (bg)).

**Labels used in this evaluation.**
- **H1 to H5** are hypotheses: the five parts into which this evaluation breaks the article's claim, each to be tested against the evidence. They are:
  - H1, intent: Netanyahu is trying to evade the election results;
  - H2, capability: he controls the security agencies;
  - H3, mechanism: the Shin Bet chief's allegations against Arab MKs (Members of the Knesset, Israel's parliament) are the means;
  - H4, constraint: only the attorney general and the Shin Bet chief's clumsiness stand in the way;
  - H5, consequence: this is Israel's worst constitutional crisis.
- **P1 to P5** are the project's predictions, each with the result that would refute it, recorded on 5 November 2026 before they resolve: the swearing-in, the certification of results, the investigation, the attorney general, and the Shin Bet chief's tenure. P1, P2 and P4 test H3, H1 and H4. P3 and P5 record two further claims in the article.

## 2. The article's claim, broken into parts

The project reads Medina's sentence as several separate claims, each of which could be right or wrong on its own. The article's claim is broken up the same way, into five hypotheses (H):

- **H1, intent:** Netanyahu is "actively trying to hold a regime coup", evading recognition of the results. `[analysis: Harel]`
- **H2, capability:** he has taken control of the security agencies, "particularly the Shin Bet and police". `[analysis: Harel]`
- **H3, mechanism:** the Shin Bet chief's foreign-funding allegations against Arab MKs are the means, and his "next move could be an attempt to thwart the swearing-in". `[analysis: Harel]`
- **H4, constraint:** the only obstacles left are the attorney general's determination and Zini's clumsiness. `[analysis: Harel]`
- **H5, consequence:** the worst constitutional crisis in Israel's history, with democracy "fighting for its life". `[analysis: Harel]`

## 3. Evaluation under Capability × Intent and the project's readings

- **Intent (H1, the claim that Netanyahu is trying to evade the results) is inferred from indirect signs, not shown.**
  - The article cites no act or statement by Netanyahu, who "has not appeared in public for more than a week". `[press: Harel]`
  - The evidence is Channel 14's broadcasts, MKs and activists "affiliated with" him, and Likud's reported dissatisfaction with Zini, itself as reported by Channel 14.
  - The project found the same gap in Medina's sentence: indications of an aim standing in for an aim established by acts (p. 685).
  - On the project's rule, a claim resting on such signs is a lead, not an established fact, until an act or a record confirms it. `[conjectural]`
- **Effective capability (H2, the claim that he controls the security agencies) looks low on the article's own facts.** "Effective" capability means what an actor can do once others push back, as distinct from formal capability, what it could do unopposed.
  - The election committee certified the results. `[press: Harel]`
  - Only a limited joint investigation was opened. `[press: Harel]`
  - The attorney general and the police restated a criminal standard of evidence for any inquiry. `[press, anonymous: Harel]`
  - Two senior Shin Bet officials confronted Zini. `[press, anonymous: Harel]`
  - Zini was quoted as saying he would not move from the attorney general's instructions. `[press: Harel, citing Channel 12]`

  The first two facts are on the record, and the next two rest on unnamed sources. All of them sit against the article's claim that Netanyahu controls the agencies (H2). `[conjectural]`
- **What survives is a standing weakness in the system, not an act.**
  - The project reads the threat in several ways. One, its "structural reading", treats a threat as a standing property of the system rather than as one actor's capability and intent at a given moment.
  - The anonymous source's point, that outside oversight of the Shin Bet is "very limited" and one decision by its chief could "completely change the rules of democracy", is a claim of that kind. `[press, anonymous: Harel]`
  - It also fits the project's "model conjecture". A conjecture, in the project's terms, is a version of a claim stated so that evidence could prove it wrong. This one holds that the plain Capability × Intent formula misdescribes the Israeli case. In Israel, what an actor is able to do and where the system is open to attack are the same fact seen from two sides. A formula that multiplies one actor's capability by its intent at one moment misses that standing weakness. `[conjectural]`
- **Net reading.** High attributed intent, low effective capability now, and a standing structural weakness. In the project's terms that is a threat in the structural sense, not an attempt established on the evidence. `[conjectural]`
- **Which rival explanation the case fits.**
  - The project keeps rival explanations of what the 2023 plan was for. Three of them bear on this case:
    - R1, narrow self-interest, such as keeping office or protecting the prime minister in his criminal trial;
    - R3, transforming the state's constitutional identity, which is Medina's own account;
    - R4, concentrating power whatever the content.
  - The article's own account is power retention after an election, which is R1. Its means is targeting Arab MKs and Arab voters, the first of the aims Medina lists as concerns (p. 685).
  - T2 is the project's planned catalogue of the government's moves, meant to tell these rivals apart. As first written, it classed each move by what the move acts on, in a fixed order with identity first. So a move against Arab MKs made to keep power would have counted toward R3.
  - This case exposed that flaw. T2 has since been restated to code each move's target and its aim separately, and to decide the rivals by aim (the note's change log, item (be); [issue 23](https://github.com/PeterJemley/israel-judicial-review-threat-model/issues/23)). `[conjectural]`
- **Medina's claim about democracy (the project's Claim A).** That claim is that the 2023 plan would put at imminent risk the right to vote, equal citizenship and a minimal separation of powers (p. 685). The case engages the right to vote. It is new evidence on that kind of threat. It is not a test of the project's conjecture about the 2023 plan, which concerns what enacting the plan would do; most of the plan was never enacted. `[conjectural]`
- **Medina's claim about the state's survival (Claim B).** Claim B is that losing those protections threatens the state itself. The project's test for it, T3, counts only policy acts by foreign governments (sanctions, aid decisions), not statements or forecasts. The article's passage on Israel as "a toxic brand" and possible sanctions in Congress is a forecast, so T3 does not count it. `[conjectural]`
- **Medina's claim about the liberal elite (Claim C), first half.** At p. 684 Medina defends the Court's doctrine as not a device for preserving a liberal elite's hegemony. The project tests that with T1. T1 codes the warning signs in each challenged Basic Law, without the coders being shown how the Court ruled, and then compares the signs with the rulings. Basic Laws are the statutes that together serve Israel in place of a written constitution. This case involves no Basic Law and no ruling, so it falls outside T1's cases and does not touch that half. `[conjectural]`
- **Claim C, second half.** The p. 685 sentence itself says the threats "are not threats to private interests or ideologies of the liberal elite", but to the right to vote, equal citizenship and a minimal separation of powers. In this evaluation the case is counted as evidence on this half, at the project author's direction (5 November 2026). It is not applied to the note. It is press-sourced and exploratory, meaning assessed after the facts were known, so it cannot count as a test. It is also not a result of T2, which has not been run.
  - The reported moves are aimed at the standing of Arab MKs and at the votes of Arab citizens after a certified election:
    - a security-service inquiry into foreign funding of Arab MKs, whose focus on Arab MKs rests on unnamed sources `[press, anonymous: Harel]`;
    - broadcasts framing the result as "Ballot Box Terror", on the record `[press: Harel]`;
    - claims of voter fraud among Israel's Arab public, in Harel's characterization `[analysis: Harel]`.
  - That is a minority that is not the liberal elite, and the right at stake is the first on Medina's p. 685 list.
  - On the facts reported, partly from unnamed sources, the episode is the kind of threat the sentence describes. `[conjectural]`
  - Two qualifications travel with it:
    - The aim may be power retention (rival R1, narrow self-interest) rather than transforming the state's constitutional identity (rival R3, Medina's account). So it evidences a threat to the franchise more than a change of identity.
    - The acts are not shown to be directed by Netanyahu or the cabinet. The actors are his political allies, broadcasters, and the Shin Bet chief, who heads a government agency. The article's intent claim (H1) rests on indirect signs.
- **The elite critique's reply on framing.** The critique Medina answers holds that the Court serves a liberal elite. He cites Ran Hirschl and Ruth Gavison for it (his fn. 4).
  - The source here is an analysis piece in Haaretz, the outlet most identified with that elite. On the critique's own terms, the framing is the elite's.
  - The project's rule answers part of this:
    - only reported facts confirmed by a second outlet or an official record are to be relied on;
    - claims from unnamed sources are leads;
    - Harel's own characterizations (a "regime coup", "propaganda outlet", "lackeys") are tagged as analysis and carry no evidential weight here.
  - The facts that carry the p. 685 point are those listed under the second half of Claim C above. They should be checked against a second outlet before the note relies on them. `[conjectural]`
- **Sohlberg, and one version of the elite critique** (at the project author's direction, 5 November 2026; recorded, not tested).
  - The project names, but does not test, an "enacting-bloc" version of the critique: that the Court acts against whichever bloc governs, whatever a law's content.
  - Here a Supreme Court justice, as chair of the Central Elections Committee, first said the election's integrity had been maintained (28 October). Then, without consulting the attorney general, he instructed the Shin Bet chief to investigate suspicions of foreign funding (29 October). Allies of the government were already advancing such claims. His committee later certified the result (4 November). `[press: Harel]` `[analysis: Harel]` for the allies' claims.
  - On that report, one justice, acting as committee chair and not in a ruling, did not act against the governing bloc. That qualifies any reading of the enacting-bloc version as a claim about the Court's members acting as one. `[conjectural]`
  - It is an act outside a judgment, so it is not one of T1's cases and is no test of the critique. `[conjectural]`

## 4. What the project's catalogue of threats lacks for this case (not applied)

The note's ontology is its catalogue of the threat's parts:
- actors;
- the instruments they use (a Basic Law amendment, control of judicial appointments, non-compliance with rulings);
- the protected values attacked;
- the mechanisms by which an instrument harms a value;
- the constraints that block an instrument.

Its matrices cross these. One of them, M3, shows which constraints check which instruments.

This case shows four gaps. At the project author's direction they are listed only, not added. The case is also not counted under T4, the planned test of whether the governing coalition switches to a new instrument after the Court blocks one. Any definition of such a switch written now, after this case was seen, and applied to it would be exploratory.
- **Instrument:** use of the security and intelligence services (investigation powers, intelligence presented to the election authority).
- **Mechanism:** delegitimizing an electorate, or contesting a certified result.
- **Constraint:** resistance inside an agency (the Shin Bet's investigations chief and legal adviser). This is distinct both from the horizontal checks other state institutions apply (the attorney general, the Court) and from the vertical ones applied from outside the state (voters, protest, foreign governments).
- **A constraint row in M3:** no existing constraint row covers the security-service instrument. The election committee, chaired by a Supreme Court justice, both certified the result and its chair instructed the investigation, so its place as a constraint is mixed.

So a refuted prediction shows that the possibility did not happen, not that the article said it would. Each refuter is fixed now. Results will be recorded in an addendum with the sources read, and anything assessed only after the fact will be labelled exploratory.

**P1. The swearing-in.** (The article's mechanism claim, H3: "Netanyahu's next move could be an attempt to thwart the swearing-in of the Knesset and the appointment of a new speaker".)
- **Supported if:** by the end of Tuesday 10 November 2026, the swearing-in is postponed, or a formal step to delay or prevent it is taken by the government, the outgoing speaker, Likud or a coalition faction. A formal step means a petition to a court, a request to the Central Elections Committee, a government or presidium decision, or a refusal to convene.
- **Refuted if:** the 26th Knesset is sworn in on 10 November 2026 and no such formal step is taken.
- **The election of a new speaker** is recorded separately, with the date and any formal step to delay it.

**P2. The certification.** (The article's intent claim, H1: "Evading recognition of election results".)
- **Supported if:** by 30 November 2026, Likud, a coalition faction or the government formally asks the Central Elections Committee or a court to annul, reopen or suspend the certified results, in whole or in part.
- **Refuted if:** no such formal request is made by that date.
- **Not counted:** statements, broadcasts and calls by individual MKs or commentators. They are recorded, but they are not a formal request.

**P3. The investigation.** Zini's line: a "strategic attack", "thousands of people", billions of shekels. The attorney general's line: a limited criminal inquiry, opened only on "reasonable suspicion of criminal offenses".
- **Zini's line supported if:** by 31 December 2026, any one of these happens:
  - the joint investigation formally extends to more than one MK;
  - it recommends charges against any MK;
  - the Shin Bet opens a security-grounds investigation of an MK.
- **The attorney general's line supported if:** by that date at least one report on the investigation exists and none of those three things has happened.
- **Unknown:** if nothing is reported, the result is recorded as unknown.

**P4. The constraint.** (The article's constraint claim, H4: the only obstacles left are the attorney general and Zini's clumsiness.)
- **Supported if:** by 31 December 2026, the attorney general is dismissed, suspended or overridden in this matter, or the Shin Bet acts against her instructions in it, as reported by two outlets or an official record.
- **Refuted if:** neither happens by that date.

**P5. A conditional prediction, for the record.** The opposition called for Zini's immediate dismissal if it forms a government without Netanyahu's coalition. Supported if he is dismissed within 60 days of that government's swearing-in. Not tested if no such government is formed.

**What each outcome would teach**
- Suppose P1 (the swearing-in) and P2 (the certification) are both refuted, P3 supports the attorney general's line, and P4 is refuted. Then:
  - the article's claim that Netanyahu controls the security agencies (H2) finds no support in events, because the agencies' own process held;
  - what survives is the structural reading: a standing weakness in oversight that did not become an act.
- If P1 or P2 is supported, the article's intent claim (H1) gains its first evidence from acts rather than indirect signs.
- If P4 is supported, the one remaining check by another state institution that the article names (H4) failed. That would be the case for which the catalogue's missing instrument would be needed.
