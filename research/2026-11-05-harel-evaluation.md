# Harel (Haaretz, 4 November 2026) evaluated through the project, with predictions recorded before they resolve

**Source.** Amos Harel, "Netanyahu Is Plotting a Final Power Grab for Israel's Democracy. His Loyalist Shin Bet Chief Is the Key", Haaretz (English), "Analysis", 4 November 2026, 18:40 IST. Read in full from a copy the author saved on 5 November 2026 at 07:52; the copy is not committed.

**Status.** A research note only, at the author's direction (5 November 2026). Nothing here is applied to the note. Claims from unnamed sources are leads until a second outlet or an official record confirms them.

**What "the project" is.** This repository tests a sentence in Barak Medina's 2026 article in I·CON (p. 685): that the 2023 judicial plan posed "an imminent risk to Israel's existence as a democracy, and, in fact, to Israel's very existence", and that these are "not threats to private interests or ideologies of the liberal elite". It reads that sentence through the threat-assessment formula Threat = Capability × Intent. The main document, "the note", is [medina-threat-conjecture-note.md](../medina-threat-conjecture-note.md); the [README](../README.md) is the plain-language entry point. Labels the project uses are explained below where they first appear.

**Tags.**

- `[press, anonymous: Harel]`: attributed in the article to unnamed sources.
- `[analysis: Harel]`: the author's own characterization.

- The committee's chair, Justice Noam Sohlberg, said Shin Bet intelligence "allegedly indicates" foreign money flowing into Israel, "originating from unknown parties with undisclosed and even hostile interests", involving "thousands of people, perhaps even more". `[press: Harel]`
- One day after the election, Sohlberg and Shin Bet chief David Zini both said electoral integrity was maintained. `[press: Harel]`
- "Last Thursday" (29 October), Sohlberg sent a public letter instructing Zini to investigate suspicions of foreign funding, without consulting the attorney general. `[press: Harel]`
- On the weekend before the election, the Shin Bet told the attorney general it had information on "a few hundred thousand shekels from a foreign country" reaching one or two Arab MKs. `[press, anonymous: Harel]`

  - Zini presented claims "without a unified and convincing line";

  - he called the suspicions a "strategic attack" and hinted at billions of shekels;
  - participants called some claims "delusional, conspiratorial". `[press, anonymous: Harel]`
- A limited investigation was opened by a joint team of the Shin Bet, police and State Attorney. `[press: Harel]`
- The Shin Bet's chief of investigations and its legal adviser confronted Zini. `[press, anonymous: Harel]`
- Channel 14 has broadcast "Ballot Box Terror" segments since the election. `[press: Harel]`
- Channel 12 quoted Zini, "in closed conversations": "I will not move an inch from the attorney general's instructions – and I will not let the country burn." `[press: Harel, citing Channel 12]`
- The swearing-in of the 26th Knesset and the choice of a speaker are due "this coming Tuesday" (10 November). The opposition has called for Zini's dismissal if it forms a government. `[press: Harel]`

## 2. The article's claim, broken into parts

The project read Medina's sentence as several separate claims, each of which could be right or wrong on its own. The article's claim is broken up the same way:

- **H1, intent:** Netanyahu is "actively trying to hold a regime coup", evading recognition of the results. `[analysis: Harel]`
- **H2, capability:** he has taken control of the security agencies, "particularly the Shin Bet and police". `[analysis: Harel]`
- **H3, mechanism:** the Shin Bet chief's foreign-funding allegations against Arab MKs are the means, and the next move may be to thwart the swearing-in. `[analysis: Harel]`
- **H4, constraint:** the only obstacles left are the attorney general's determination and Zini's clumsiness. `[analysis: Harel]`
- **H5, consequence:** the worst constitutional crisis in Israel's history, with democracy "fighting for its life". `[analysis: Harel]`

## 3. Evaluation under Capability × Intent and the project's readings

- **Intent (H1) is attributed through proxies, not shown.**
  - The article cites no act or statement by Netanyahu, who "has not appeared in public for more than a week".
  - The evidence is Channel 14's broadcasts, MKs and activists "affiliated with" him, and Likud's reported dissatisfaction with Zini, itself as reported by Channel 14.
  - The project found the same gap in Medina's sentence: indications of an aim standing in for an aim established by acts (p. 685).
  - On the project's rule, a claim resting on proxies is a lead, not an established fact, until an act or a record confirms it. `[conjectural]`
- **Effective capability (H2) is low on the article's own facts.** "Effective" capability means what an actor can do once others push back, as distinct from formal capability, what it could do unopposed.
  - The committee certified the results.
  - The attorney general imposed a criminal evidentiary standard.
  - Only a limited joint investigation opened.
  - Officials inside the Shin Bet resisted.
  - Zini undertook to follow the attorney general.

  The body reports checks that held, which sits against the headline's claim of capture. `[conjectural]`
- **What survives is a standing weakness in the system, not an act.**
  - The project reads the threat in several ways. One, its "structural reading", treats a threat as a standing property of the system rather than as one actor's capability and intent at a given moment.
  - The anonymous source's point is a claim of that kind: oversight of the Shin Bet is "very limited – and one decision made by the Shin Bet chief is enough to completely change the rules of democracy".
  - It also fits one of the project's conjectures, a version of a claim stated so that evidence could prove it wrong. This one holds that the plain Capability × Intent formula misdescribes the Israeli case, because what an actor is able to do and where the system is open to attack are the same fact seen from two sides; a formula that multiplies one actor's capability by its intent at a moment misses that standing weakness. `[conjectural]`
- **Net reading.** High attributed intent, low effective capability now, and a standing structural weakness. In the project's terms that is a threat in the structural sense, not an attempt established on the evidence. `[conjectural]`
- **Which rival explanation the case fits.**
  - The project keeps rival explanations of what the 2023 plan was for:
    - R1, narrow self-interest, such as keeping office or the prime minister's criminal trial;
    - R3, transforming the state's constitutional identity, which is Medina's own account;
    - R4, concentrating power whatever the content.
  - The article's own account is power retention after an election, which is R1. Its means is targeting Arab MKs and voters, the first of the aims Medina lists as concerns (p. 685).
  - T2 is the project's planned catalogue of the government's moves, meant to tell these rivals apart. As first written, it classed each move by what the move acts on, in a fixed order with identity first, so a move against Arab MKs made to keep power would have counted toward R3.
  - This case exposed that flaw. T2 has since been restated to code each move's target and its aim separately, and to decide the rivals by aim (the note's change log, item (be); [issue 23](https://github.com/PeterJemley/israel-judicial-review-threat-model/issues/23)). `[conjectural]`
- **Medina's claim about democracy (the project's Claim A).** That claim is that the 2023 plan would put at imminent risk the right to vote, equal citizenship and a minimal separation of powers. The case engages the right to vote. It is new evidence on that threat, not a test of the project's conjecture about the 2023 plan itself, which concerns the plan as enacted. `[conjectural]`
- **Medina's claim about the state's survival (Claim B).** The project's test for that claim, T3, counts only allied policy events (sanctions, aid decisions), not statements or forecasts. The passage on Israel as "a toxic brand" and possible congressional sanctions is speculation, so T3 does not count it. `[conjectural]`
- **Medina's claim about the liberal elite (Claim C), first half.** At p. 684 Medina defends the Court's doctrine as not a device for preserving a liberal elite's hegemony. The project tests that with T1, a blind coding of how the Supreme Court has ruled on challenged Basic Laws. This case involves no Basic Law and no ruling, so it falls outside T1's cases and does not touch that half. `[conjectural]`
- **Claim C, second half.** The p. 685 sentence itself says the threats "are not threats to private interests or ideologies of the liberal elite", but to the right to vote, equal citizenship and a minimal separation of powers. The case is counted as evidence on this half, at the author's direction (5 November 2026). It is press-sourced and exploratory, meaning assessed after the facts were known, so it cannot count as a test. It is also not a result of T2, which has not been run.
  - The reported moves are aimed at the franchise and standing of Arab MKs and voters after a certified election: a security-service inquiry into foreign funding of Arab MKs, and broadcasts framing the result as "Ballot Box Terror".
  - That is a minority that is not the liberal elite, and the right at stake is the first on Medina's p. 685 list.
  - On the facts reported on the record, the episode is the kind of threat the sentence describes. `[press: Harel]` `[conjectural]`
  - Two qualifications travel with it:
    - The aim may be power retention (R1) rather than identity transformation (R3), so it evidences a threat to the franchise more than a change of identity.
    - The reported acts are by allies, broadcasters and the Shin Bet chief, and are not shown to be the government's own (H1).
- **The elite critique's reply on framing.** The critique Medina answers holds that the Court serves a liberal elite; he cites Hirschl and Gavison for it (fn. 4).
  - The source here is an analysis piece in Haaretz, the outlet most identified with that elite. On the critique's own terms, the framing is the elite's.
  - The project's rule answers part of this:
    - only reported facts confirmed by a second outlet or an official record are to be relied on;
    - claims from unnamed sources are leads;
    - the author's characterization (a "regime coup", "propaganda outlet", "lackeys") is tagged as analysis and carries no evidential weight here.
  - The facts that carry the p. 685 point, the committee's certification and Sohlberg's letter, are on the record, and should be checked against a second outlet before the note relies on them. `[conjectural]`
- **Sohlberg, and one version of the elite critique** (at the author's direction, 5 November 2026; recorded, not tested).
  - The project names, but does not test, an "enacting-bloc" version of the critique: that the Court acts against whichever bloc governs, whatever a law's content.
  - Here a Supreme Court justice, as chair of the Central Elections Committee, certified the result. Then, without consulting the attorney general, he instructed the Shin Bet chief to investigate suspicions of foreign funding that the coalition's media were advancing. `[press: Harel]`
  - On that report, members of the Court do not act as one bloc against the government. That qualifies any reading of the enacting-bloc version as a claim about the Court as a single actor.
  - It is an act outside a judgment, so it is not one of T1's cases and is no test of the critique. `[conjectural]`

## 4. What the project's catalogue of threats lacks for this case (not applied)

The note's ontology is a catalogue of the threat's parts:
- actors;
- the instruments they use (a Basic Law amendment, control of judicial appointments, non-compliance with rulings);
- the protected values attacked;
- the mechanisms by which an instrument harms a value;
- the constraints that block an instrument.

Its matrices cross these. One of them, M3, shows which constraints check which instruments.

This case shows four gaps. At the author's direction they are listed only, not added. The case is also not counted under T4, the planned test of whether the governing coalition switches to a new instrument after the Court blocks one. A definition written now and applied to this same case would be exploratory.
- **Instrument:** use of the security and intelligence services (investigation powers, intelligence presented to the election authority).
- **Mechanism:** delegitimizing an electorate, or contesting a certified result.
- **Constraint:** resistance inside an agency (the Shin Bet's investigations chief and legal adviser). This is distinct both from the horizontal checks other state institutions apply (the attorney general, the Court) and from the vertical ones applied from outside the state (voters, protest, allies).
- **A row in M3:** no existing constraint row covers the security-service instrument. The election committee, chaired by a Supreme Court justice, both certified the result and requested the investigation, so its place as a constraint is mixed.

## 5. Predictions, recorded on 5 November 2026 before they resolve

Each prediction follows from the article's claims. Each refuter is fixed now. Results will be recorded in an addendum with the sources read, and anything assessed only after the fact will be labelled exploratory.

**P1. The swearing-in.** (H3: "Netanyahu's next move could be an attempt to thwart the swearing-in of the Knesset and the appointment of a new speaker".)
- **Supported if:** by the end of Tuesday 10 November 2026, the swearing-in is postponed, or a formal step to delay or prevent it is taken by the government, the outgoing speaker, Likud or a coalition faction. A formal step means a petition to a court, a request to the Central Elections Committee, a government or presidium decision, or a refusal to convene.
- **Refuted if:** the 26th Knesset is sworn in on 10 November 2026 and no such formal step is taken.
- **The election of a new speaker** is recorded separately, with the date and any formal step to delay it.

**P2. The certification.** (H1: "Evading recognition of election results".)
- **Supported if:** by 30 November 2026, Likud, a coalition faction or the government formally asks the Central Elections Committee or a court to annul, reopen or suspend the certified results, in whole or in part.
- **Refuted if:** no such formal request is made by that date.
- **Not counted:** statements, broadcasts and calls by individual MKs or commentators. They are recorded, but they are not a formal request.

**P3. The investigation.** (Zini's line: a "strategic attack", "thousands of people", billions of shekels. The attorney general's line: a limited criminal inquiry on reasonable suspicion.)
- **Zini's line supported if:** by 31 December 2026, the joint investigation formally extends to more than one MK, or recommends charges against any MK, or the Shin Bet opens a security-grounds investigation of an MK.
- **The attorney general's line supported if:** by that date the investigation stays limited, with no charge recommended against an MK.
- **Unknown:** if nothing is reported, the result is recorded as unknown.

**P4. The constraint (H4).**
- **Supported if:** by 31 December 2026, the attorney general is dismissed, suspended or overridden in this matter, or the Shin Bet acts against her instructions in it, as reported by two outlets or an official record.
- **Refuted if:** neither happens by that date.

**P5. A conditional prediction, for the record.** If a government is formed without Netanyahu's coalition, the opposition said it would dismiss Zini "immediately". Supported if he is dismissed within 60 days of that government's swearing-in. Not tested if no such government is formed.

**What each outcome would teach**
- If P1 and P2 are both refuted, while P3 and P4 favour the attorney general's line:
  - the article's claim that the security services have been captured (H2) fails on events;
  - what survives is the structural reading: a standing weakness in oversight that did not become an act.
- If P1 or P2 is supported: H1 gains its first evidence from acts, as distinct from proxies.
- If P4 is supported: the one remaining check by another state institution named in H4 failed. That would be the case for which the catalogue's missing instrument would be needed.
