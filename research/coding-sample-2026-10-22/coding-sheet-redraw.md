# Coding sheet for the comparison sample, redraw, 2026-10-22

You are one of two independent coders. Code every enactment in the batch assigned to you on the variables defined below, from the permitted sources only. Record, for every cell, the value, the source (URL or document) and a short quotation or data point that supports it. Where no permitted source settles a cell, leave it blank and say what you looked for. Do not infer a value from what seems likely. These enactments were never challenged before the Court, so you will not find judgments on them; the rules below still apply to any court case that applied or interpreted them.

## Rules of the sheet

- Permitted sources: the enactment's text as published (Hebrew Wikisource or Nevo copies of the gazette text, or the Knesset's gazette PDF through the Internet Archive capture given below); Knesset voting data (the extracts below, drawn from the Knesset's vote tables as mirrored by Open Knesset, or press reports or the Knesset record of the recorded tally where the extracts do not cover the enactment); records of the government's composition on the day of the vote (the Knesset's own records, or an encyclopedia article on that government where the Knesset site is unreachable); dates of the first and final readings (Knesset records, the gazette footnote, or press reports of the readings); the bill's explanatory notes where reachable. The NYU Faculty Digital Archive mirrors the Knesset record (Divrei HaKnesset) for many years; the Knesset's legislation database (titles, publication, bill references) is mirrored at production.oknesset.org/pipelines/data/laws/.
- Forbidden sources: any court judgment, any translation or summary of a judgment, any article or page whose subject is litigation about the enactment, any commentary that discusses how a court treated it, and any file in the repository this sheet comes from other than this sheet. If a page you open turns out to be about a court case, close it and do not use anything from it. Say in your notes if that happened.
- Do not use your own recollection of how any of these enactments fared in court or in politics. Code the enactment as a text and a legislative event only.
- Identify enactments by the titles and sequence numbers below. Do not add case names.
- Work in a fresh subfolder of your scratch directory; do not list or open other folders there.

- The Knesset's plenum-vote table (kns_plenumvote), its per-member results (kns_plenumvoteresult) and its member-position table (kns_persontoposition), mirrored by Open Knesset at production.oknesset.org/pipelines/data/knesset/ and .../members/, are Knesset voting data and may be read directly; they cover October 2003 onward.

## Variables

- `named_beneficiary`: Yes if the enactment addresses a specific interest of the sitting government or an identified officeholder, from its text and explanatory notes; otherwise No. Name the beneficiary if Yes.
- `immediate_effect`: Deferred if the text fixes effect after the next general election, or more than twelve months after publication; otherwise Yes, whether the text states effect on publication, on an earlier date (retroactive effect included), or within twelve months, or states no commencement clause at all. Record the basis: "stated", "retroactive", "within twelve months", or "by absence of a clause". Deferred takes precedence where both could apply.
- `coalition_only`: Yes if no member of an opposition faction voted for the enactment at its final reading; No if at least one did; blank if the vote record is unavailable. Record the tally and any cross-party votes by name and faction, and which factions were in the coalition on the day of the vote with the source for that.
- `expedited`: Yes if the final vote came within thirty days of the first reading, or if a special procedure shortened the committee stage; No otherwise. Record the dates of first reading and final vote and the number of days.
- `scope`: Yes if the enactment removes a check on the government or a protected right, or renders it practically inoperative, and in either case provides no alternative mechanism; No otherwise. A one-time exemption from a rule is No. Say which check or right, and whether an alternative mechanism exists, in your own words from the text.
- `temporary_provision`: Yes if the enactment was passed as a temporary provision or applies to a single instance; No otherwise.
- `targets_judiciary`: Yes if the enactment's subject is judicial review, judicial appointments or judicial independence; No otherwise.
- `deviation_tier`: None if the enactment's text engages none of the five protected values (free and equal elections; core human rights and equality; separation of powers; rule of law; an independent judiciary); Entrenches if it entrenches one or more of them and contradicts none; Limited if it contradicts one to a limited extent; Sharp if it contradicts one sharply. Record which values are engaged and which is the principal subject. Contradiction takes precedence over entrenchment, and the more severe contradiction over the less.
- `direction`: Illiberal if the text or stated purpose moves the constitutional order toward preferring Jewish identity over equal citizenship, restricts the rights of minorities or women, or narrows judicial review or judicial independence; Liberal if it moves the other way on any of those; Neutral if it does none of these, which includes fiscal, procedural and electoral housekeeping measures. Record every axis engaged and code from the principal subject.
- `final_vote_record`: for the one-vote check: the date, tally and identifier (vote number, Knesset record reference or press report) of the enactment's final-reading vote, and whether that vote also approved any other law. If you cannot find a final vote, say so.
