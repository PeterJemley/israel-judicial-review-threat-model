### The signs of abuse

- **Answer:** No source I opened shows the Court listing the project’s four signs, as a set or as a presumption. Secondary accounts describe a two-stage test from *Shafir*. First, the Court asks whether the norm has the features of a constitutional norm, using a non-exhaustive list of stability, generality and coherence with the constitutional framework. Second, if the norm fails, the respondents may justify it.
- **How the four signs compare:**
  - A specific interest of the current government corresponds most closely to generality. Dishon reports Justice Hendel treating personalised provisions as a mark of misuse, at 424.
  - Immediate effect appears in a scholar’s proposal. Bloch suggests that immediate or retrospective amendments should create a presumption that they are personal. That is her own proposal, offered as prior art for your project, and she does not attribute it to the Court.
  - Support only from coalition members and expedited passage did not appear in any account of the test that I read.
  - Dishon offers his own list of signifiers for temporary amendments, at 421–24: a weak constitutional setting, repeated extension, vague reasoning and personalised provisions. That list is his, and it is confined to temporary amendments.
- **Source:** Yochai Rosner, *Five Approaches to Constituent Power: Reflections on the Israeli Reasonableness Ruling*, 27 German L.J. 216, 227 (2026) (citing *Shafir* at 31–33); Ofra Bloch, *Generality and Abusive Constitutionalism*, 85 Md. L. Rev. 112, 133–35 (2025) (citing *Shafir* paras. 36–44); both Lexis.
- **Quotation:** “carries the characteristics or features of a constitutional norm” (Rosner at 227, quoting *Shafir*); “should create a presumption of personality” (Bloch at 135, her own proposal)
- **Tag:** [attested: Lexis, 27 German L.J. 216, 227; 85 Md. L. Rev. 112, 133, 135]
- **Inference:** Shifting the justification to the respondents works somewhat like a presumption. Neither source uses that word for the Court’s test. [conjectural]

## Q9. Prior-art checks in the full text

### (a) Roznai, Dixon & Landau, 56 Isr. L. Rev. 292 (2023)

- **Answer:** Yes on both points. At 302 they say a law’s effect may be prima facie evidence of the intent behind it, and they add that the effect can also be a stand-alone measure.
